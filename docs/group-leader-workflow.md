# Proposed group leader workflow

October 17, 2026; expanded October 18, 2026. This walkthrough complements the
[coordinator workflow](coordinator-workflow.md),
[volunteer workflow](volunteer-workflow.md) and [feature list](feature-list.md).
Group signup links, readiness tracking, booking changes and group leader permissions
are confirmed feature scope. Detailed interfaces and policies below are proposals,
not implemented behavior or separately approved requirements.

The leader's path is: request places, receive a reservation, invite participants,
monitor readiness, manage changes and coordinate participation.

## 1. Find an activity and request places

Example: a company wants to bring ten employees to a Saturday food distribution.
The leader reviews availability, roles, requirements and expectations, then submits
the group name, contact, requested places and relevant needs. Keep the booking
request distinct from a confirmed reservation. The public discovery interface
remains a proposal.

## 2. Receive the reservation

After coordinator confirmation, show the date, location, reserved roles, capacity
and registration deadline. Explain the policy for releasing unused places. Group
membership is distinct from event participation; the organizer may not attend.

## 3. Invite participants

Share the group signup link. Each participant registers using their own account
and completes their own applicable requirements and signatures. Their place counts
against the reservation rather than consuming an additional place. Sharing a
signup link does not confer leader permissions or grant roster access.

## 4. Track readiness

A proposed dashboard for the company's booking could show:

| Saturday distribution | People/places |
| --- | ---: |
| Reserved places | 10 |
| Participants registered | 8 |
| Ready and confirmed | 6 |
| Requirements incomplete | 2 |
| Places unclaimed | 2 |

These counts overlap; eight registered people occupy eight of the ten reserved
places. The leader can identify whom to follow up with using only permitted
readiness information. Private documents, screening results and sensitive profile
details remain restricted. Readiness and confirmed assignment are distinct states,
even when presented together in a summary.

## 5. Prepare the group

Review arrival instructions, clothing/equipment expectations, the nonprofit contact
and any supervision requirements. Confirmed participants receive their own
calendar invitations. Operational email notifications are confirmed; additional
group communication tools remain separately proposed.

## 6. Handle changes

If someone cancels, invite a replacement into an available group place under the
organization's policy. The replacement completes applicable requirements before
confirmation. Requests to increase capacity, change dates or switch reserved roles
go to the coordinator; the existing confirmed booking remains until the change is
approved. Clarify whether changes affect one date or future recurring dates.

Preserve the change history and update affected assignments and calendar events.
Cancellation rights, deadlines and approval policies remain to be designed.

## 7. Coordinate on the day

Access the permitted group roster and report missing participants or changes.
Check-in assistance is a proposed capability requiring a separately defined
permission; no check-in method has been selected. Leader status alone does not
grant access to private evidence or coordinator authority. Follow the
[day-of workflow](day-of-workflow.md) within the leader's actual permission scope;
each participant still has their own attendance record. Direct accommodation
requests to authorized staff and sensitive concerns to the restricted incident
route. Do not copy these narratives into a shared group roster or support note.

## 8. Review participation afterward

Subject to explicit organization permissions, show attendance and the group's
approved service total. Allow discrepancies to be reported to the coordinator.
Do not grant hour approval merely because someone is a group leader; approval
requires separate authorized reviewer responsibilities. Distinguish approved hours
from pending submissions and avoid exposing unrelated personal service history.

## 9. Arrange another visit

Request another booking or recurring arrangement. Returning participants reuse
valid onboarding as applicable, while each visit retains its own reservation,
roster and attendance. Belonging to a group does not assign someone to every visit.

## Proposed leader home screen

Focus on upcoming bookings, unclaimed places, people needing action and coordinator
updates. Messaging interfaces and layout remain proposals. Leader appointment,
removal and delegation, field-level access and day-of permissions require detailed
design before implementation.
