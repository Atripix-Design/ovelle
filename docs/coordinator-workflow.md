# Proposed coordinator workflow

October 17, 2026. This walkthrough combines the [confirmed feature list](feature-list.md)
with proposed interaction details informed by [organization examples](research/volunteer-operations.md).
It describes intended product behavior, not an implemented application. Interface
names, automation policies and reporting details remain open for design review.

## Example: a weekly food distribution

A coordinator runs a Saturday distribution needing 12 packers, two drivers and
one team leader. A local company reserves six packer places. The activity repeats
weekly, but volunteers may make either one-off or recurring commitments.

## 1. Prepare the organization

Set up programs, locations, coordinator responsibilities and document templates.
Configure onboarding and requirements for the roles the organization actually
uses. For example, packers need an orientation and waiver; drivers may need an
additional organization-defined qualification. Do not impose screening on every
role by default.

Set up calendar invitation delivery and operational email notifications. Providers,
templates and schedules remain to be designed. Payment account setup is optional.

## 2. Create the activity and shifts

Enter the location, time zone, schedule, instructions, contact and role capacities.
Create the weekly series and review its occurrences. Reserve six of the 12 packer
places for the company, leaving six packer places available outside that group.
Set a proposed reservation deadline and identify the group contact.

Opportunity publishing is confirmed, including drafts and public/invitation-only
visibility. Reusable templates and website embeds remain proposals.

## 3. Recruit and onboard

Individuals register for opportunities; the group contact shares the
group signup link with coworkers. Each participant completes their own applicable
requirements. The coordinator can distinguish six reserved places from, for
example, four named participants, three completed registrations and two confirmed
assignments. These counts overlap; they must not be summed as separate places.

The coordinator reviews outstanding submissions and follows up with people who
need help. A group leader sees the participation information needed for their
group without access to private evidence or unrelated volunteers.

## 4. Confirm assignments

Review each role's vacancies, participant availability and eligibility on the
service date. Resolve pending requirements before confirming an assignment that
requires them. Capture a one-off or recurring commitment as appropriate.

Send calendar invitations for confirmed assignments. Calendar acceptance does not
prove attendance, complete onboarding or create extra capacity. Define how a
calendar decline is reconciled with an Ovelle assignment before implementation.

## 5. Prepare for the next occurrence

Proposed coordinator home view: upcoming shifts and items requiring action,
including unfilled roles, expiring requirements, incomplete group registrations,
replacement offers and attendance awaiting review.

Review the roster, release unused group reservations under the agreed policy,
and share final instructions. Printable rosters and email reminders are confirmed
scope; their interfaces and delivery schedules remain open. A staffing total must
still reveal a missing required driver or team leader.

## 6. Handle changes and replacement coverage

If a driver cancels for one Saturday, preserve the recurring commitment for other
dates. Reopen that occurrence's driver place, review eligible replacements and
send an offer through the agreed channel. A sent offer does not fill the vacancy.

On acceptance, confirm within capacity and send the replacement's calendar
invitation; cancel or update the original assignment's invitation. If no one
accepts, keep the gap visible for coordinator action. Rules for offer deadlines
and automatic offers remain open.

For an activity change, specify whether it affects one occurrence or future
occurrences, then update the relevant assignments and calendar events.

## 7. Run the activity

Proposed day-of view: assigned people, roles, readiness and attendance. Staff-assisted
or QR check-in methods are not yet selected. Record attendance and actual service
time, including late arrivals, early departures and no-shows. Define how approved
walk-ins consume capacity and satisfy role requirements before supporting them.

Decide offline access and later synchronization explicitly before implementation;
this walkthrough does not establish that capability.

## 8. Reconcile attendance and hours

Review missing or disputed records. For example, correct a person incorrectly
marked absent or adjust an early departure. Preserve the previous record and
the correction's author, time and reason.

Separate recorded/submitted time from coordinator-approved hours. Planned duration
does not become verified service automatically. Define when corrections require
new approval and how reports or previously issued statements reflect amendments.

## 9. Close out and prepare the next shift

Proposed reporting shows filled/vacant roles, attendance, no-shows, approved hours
and program-specific outcomes with separate units. Identify follow-up needs and
prepare the next occurrence without duplicating completed onboarding.

Donations and payments run alongside this workflow: associate transactions with
the relevant campaign/program, reconcile provider status, and track refunds and
fees. Volunteering does not require making a donation. Reports must keep received
money, pledges, service hours and program outcomes distinct.

## Decisions still needed

- Default registration, review and assignment policies by program or role.
- Group reservation deadlines, leader permissions and release behavior.
- Recurrence boundaries, time-zone changes and calendar-response reconciliation.
- Replacement selection, availability conflicts and offer expiry.
- Day-of entry methods, offline behavior and correction approval rules.
- Reporting definitions and scope of email communications beyond invitations.
