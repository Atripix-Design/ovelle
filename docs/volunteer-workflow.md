# Proposed volunteer workflow

October 17, 2026; expanded October 18, 2026. This walkthrough complements the
[coordinator workflow](coordinator-workflow.md) and [feature list](feature-list.md).
It describes intended behavior, not implemented functionality. Hour submissions,
coordinator approval, correction requests and service history are confirmed feature
scope; interface labels and other details below remain proposed unless separately
confirmed in the feature list.

The volunteer's path is: find an opportunity, complete requirements, receive an
assignment, attend, submit actual service time and receive a review decision.

## 1. Find an opportunity

Open a group signup link or a proposed nonprofit volunteer page. Review the
activity, location, schedule, available roles and requirements before registering.
Example: join a Saturday food distribution as a packer. Public signup pages and
embeds remain proposed delivery methods.

## 2. Create an account or sign in

Provide basic details and relevant interests or skills. Returning volunteers reuse
their profile and valid documents within the organization, subject to applicable
role requirements and renewals.

## 3. Complete role-specific onboarding

See a checklist of applicable steps: waiver, orientation, training or qualification
review. Each item shows what needs action, awaits review or is complete. Explain
the next step without requiring documents unrelated to the chosen role.

## 4. Choose a shift or recurring commitment

Request a one-off place or a recurring schedule. Group participants register
against their group's reserved capacity. Pending requirements or coordinator
review remain visible; requesting a place does not imply confirmed assignment.

## 5. Receive confirmation

After assignment, see the confirmed date, role, arrival instructions and contact.
Receive a calendar invitation. Calendar acceptance does not establish attendance,
complete onboarding or create another assignment.

## 6. Manage commitments

View confirmed shifts and pending requests. Cancel one occurrence without removing
future recurring commitments. If offered replacement coverage, explicitly accept
before the role is assigned, subject to eligibility and available capacity.

## 7. Attend and record actual participation

Follow arrival instructions and get staff-assisted check-in/out without requiring
a personal phone. Record actual participation, including late arrivals, early
departures and applicable breaks. Report a missing checkout for clarification
rather than assuming the scheduled duration will be approved. The
[day-of workflow](day-of-workflow.md) proposes a controlled paper fallback;
QR entry, automatic tracking and offline app synchronization remain undecided.

## 8. Review and submit hours

Review the activity date and recorded service time. Explain missing or incorrect
time, for example, "I helped with cleanup until 1 p.m." Submit for coordinator
review and see an awaiting-approval status. Prevent duplicate counting across
revisions. Scheduled shift duration does not automatically become approved hours.

## 9. Receive a review decision and request corrections

See whether time is approved or needs clarification and respond to questions.
If a coordinator adjusts the time, show the approved amount and explanation.
Provide a correction-request action for attendance or hours, including previously
reviewed records. Retain the request and its decision history; a pending correction
does not silently alter approved totals.

Detailed rejection, withdrawal, resubmission and reopening policies remain open.
Keep submission review, correction-request status and attendance separate so that
one ambiguous label does not conceal what actually happened.

## 10. View service history

View past activities and participation with recorded, submitted and approved time
clearly distinguished. Keep pending submissions separate from the approved total.
Show review explanations and resolved or outstanding correction requests.
Downloadable verification statements remain proposed, not confirmed scope.

## 11. Get support or request an accommodation

Request help or ask staff to record a phone/in-person request. See appropriate
updates, the responsible contact and whether a response is needed. Routine support
has its own follow-up and resolution; confidential staff notes need separately
defined visibility. A missed shift can prompt a conversation without automatically
canceling future commitments.

Optionally explain an adjustment needed for participation, without being required
to provide a diagnosis. Review the agreed arrangement, dates and responsible
contact; a submitted request is not confirmation it can be provided. Decide what
practical information relevant staff may see. A group leader does not automatically
receive the request or its supporting evidence.

## 12. Report a concern

Use a separate restricted incident route for unsafe situations or inappropriate
behavior. Show immediate on-site/emergency instructions separately; an online
report is not emergency dispatch. Define an alternate recipient if the concern
involves the usual coordinator. Explain status visibility and limits of
confidentiality without exposing other people's evidence or investigation notes.

## Proposed volunteer home screen

| Area | Information |
| --- | --- |
| Next shift | Date, location, role and arrival instructions. |
| Action needed | Incomplete onboarding, clarification requests and replacement offers. |
| Upcoming commitments | Confirmed assignments and pending requests with distinct statuses. |
| My hours | Approved total, pending submissions and service history. |
| Support and arrangements | Own support requests, agreed accommodations and appropriate updates; a separate concern-reporting route. |

Always distinguish signup, confirmed assignment, attendance and approved hours.
Each stage should make the volunteer's next step clear.
